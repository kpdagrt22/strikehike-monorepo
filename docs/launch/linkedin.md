I tested three market legends against 99 years of the S&P 500. None showed a
meaningful difference.

Mercury retrograde, the full moon and eclipses all get blamed for moving markets.
So I measured them the same way: the mean in-window daily return against every
other day, with a 2,000-draw permutation test on the daily S&P 500 record
(1928–2026).

Mercury retrograde — 0.48%/day in vs 0.29%/day out, p = 0.62
Full moon — 0.08% in vs 0.09% out, p = 0.91
Eclipses — 0.12% in vs −0.07% out, p = 0.25

All three come back the same way: no meaningful difference in this record. I
publish the misses, not just the hits — the method, the windows and every number
sit on the page.

Descriptive historical research, not advice and not a forecast. The write-up and
the chart: https://strikehike.com/tested
